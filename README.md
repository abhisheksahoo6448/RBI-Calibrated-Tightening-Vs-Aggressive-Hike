# RBI: From Calibrated Tightening to a More Hawkish Policy Stance?

### My Observation

Based on my assessment of the macroeconomic environment, I believe the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) may shift from its current calibrated tightening stance towards a more hawkish approach. I expect the MPC to increase the policy repo rate by a cumulative 75 basis points, with a 50-basis-point hike in December 2026, followed by a further 25-basis-point hike in February 2027.

### Key factors supporting my view

**1. Persistent and accelerating inflationary pressures**

Headline CPI inflation has accelerated from 4.83% in August to 5.54% in September and 6.09% in October 2026, exceeding the RBI's upper tolerance limit of 6%. Food inflation has also risen sharply, increasing from 5.97% in August to 8.94% in October.

The key drivers of inflationary pressures include:

- **Geopolitical tensions:** The US–Iran conflict and broader geopolitical instability have contributed to volatility in crude oil prices, potentially increasing India's energy import costs and creating inflationary pressures.
- **Rising food inflation:** Increasing food prices are contributing significantly to headline inflation and may influence household inflation expectations.
- **Rupee depreciation:** Continued depreciation of the INR may increase the cost of imported goods and intensify imported inflation.

**2. External monetary policy and currency pressures**

The Federal Reserve's monetary policy decisions, alongside geopolitical uncertainty and currency-market movements, may influence India's capital flows and exchange-rate stability.

A wider interest-rate differential in favour of India could improve the relative attractiveness of Indian financial assets and support foreign capital inflows, while higher domestic interest rates may also help moderate depreciation pressures on the INR. However, capital flows will continue to depend on risk-adjusted returns, investor sentiment and currency expectations.

**3. Resilient economic growth**

India's real GDP growth of 7.8% in Q1 FY27 indicates resilience in economic activity. Stronger growth may provide the RBI with some policy space to prioritize inflation control through tighter monetary conditions, although the impact of higher borrowing costs on consumption, investment and future growth will remain a key consideration.

### Policy outlook

Given the combination of accelerating inflation, elevated food prices, external uncertainties and currency depreciation pressures, I believe the balance of risks may shift towards further monetary tightening. I expect the MPC to adopt a more hawkish approach, potentially delivering a cumulative 75-basis-point increase in the repo rate by February 2027, subject to the persistence of inflationary pressures and the evolving growth outlook.
